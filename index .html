Questions I have already worked on

Screen Time, Sleep and Attention Survey

Question
Does the time students spend on screens for non-study reasons change how well they sleep and concentrate?
Method
I made an online survey and collected 27 answers. I compared 18 students aged 13 to 18 across three screen-time groups and worked out the correlation between screen time and each sleep or focus answer.
Result
No reliable link between screen time and hours slept, sleep quality or concentration. Heavier screen users reported more distraction from phone notifications, but that weakened once repeated answers were removed.
What I learned
How to design survey questions, clean messy data, and describe a result honestly when it is not what I expected.

Age-Related Brain Volume Decline Analysis

Question
Does brain volume fall as people get older, and how strong is that link?
Method
I used open brain-scan data from OASIS. The sheet had 27 rows, but 22 were the same person, so I kept one row per person (6 people, aged 18 to 73). In Python I plotted age against normalised whole-brain volume, fitted a line, and calculated the correlation and p-value.
Result
Older people had smaller brain volume (r = −0.78, p = 0.068, n = 6). With so few people this is a strong hint, not proof, and I set out the limits of the sample in the report.
What I learned
How to clean data, why one repeated person can make a result look stronger than it is, and why a small sample needs careful wording.

Bioinformatics Literature Analysis

Question
What holds the drug risperidone inside the dopamine D2 receptor, and why does its shape fit?
Method
I studied the 3D structure of the D2 receptor bound to risperidone (PDB 6CM4) from the RCSB Protein Data Bank, and compared it with how dopamine sits in the same kind of pocket, using published research.
Finding
Risperidone's positively charged nitrogen is anchored by a key aspartate, and many hydrophobic contacts with aromatic residues hold the rest. It is longer than dopamine, so it touches more amino acids and blocks dopamine from binding.
Why it matters
Dopamine affects mood, motivation and focus, and risperidone is used when dopamine signalling is overactive, as in schizophrenia. This links the biology of the brain to mental health.
